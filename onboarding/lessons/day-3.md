# Day 3: Connect your tools

Your bot can't keep a ledger of promises it can't see. Today we find every place your matters actually live, decide how much access each one gets, plug them in, and then, as a reward, your butler gives you your first morning briefing.

**Time:** about 15 minutes of interview with your bot, then 30 to 45 connecting. All in your bot's chat.

## Why it matters

Your mental load lives in six inboxes, three calendars and a school app nobody asked for. Once your butler can read them, he can warn you that the 08:30 call collides with the school run before you've had coffee. And because every tool gets an access level, nothing gets more power than it needs. Then tomorrow morning, for the first time in a while, one calm message tells you what the day is.

## Today

1. **Say yes to your bot's morning message** (or *"I'm doing the 7-day challenge, day 3"*). It already knows your matters from yesterday and opens with the tools interview.
2. **Answer its questions.** It sweeps through inboxes, calendars, Drive, chat, meeting notes (Fathom and friends), task tools, your CRM and the odd school portal. You'll be amazed how many places you keep things. Your bot won't be.
3. **Pick an access level per tool.** Read, draft, write in its own space, or write for others (which always waits for your yes). It suggests; you decide. Banking and password managers: never.
4. **Nothing to copy.** Your bot saves the list as `my-tools.md` and reads it back.
5. **Let your bot guide you through connecting**, most important first. It tries a connector your assistant actually has. If there isn't one, you paste or export. It does not invent a browser or ask you to type a password into the chat. It tests each one by reading one harmless item (your latest email subject, tomorrow's first event) before moving on.
6. **Approve your two daily routines.** A **morning briefing at 07:50** and an **evening heads-up at 21:00**. Change the times if your mornings start earlier. Your bot will offer to fold Day 1's promise question into the evening one. Say yes; one evening message is plenty.
7. **Read your first briefing.** It runs one right away. Tell it what to change: *"shorter"*, *"family first"*, *"skip the weather"*.

Nobody will ever ask for your passwords: not your bot, not me. When a login is needed, you sign in on your own device, or into a secret store the product provides. Never into a chat.

## Done when

Your bot has sent its "Day 3 done" block: tools connected and tested, access levels saved, source map written, both routines approved, and you've read one briefing.

## Proof to post

A screenshot of the "Day 3 done" block, or the top three lines of your first briefing. Blur email subjects, event titles, channel and file names. The rest of us are nosy, but not that nosy.

## Snags

- **Sign-in pop-up doesn't appear.** Allow pop-ups for the site and try again. Then *"retry the ___ connection"*.
- **Wrong Google account connected.** *"Disconnect calendar"*, then reconnect with the right one.
- **Test read shows nothing.** Tomorrow may simply be empty. Lucky you. Ask for *"my next event this week"* instead.
- **WhatsApp or iMessage won't connect.** Usually they can't. Your tools list includes a workaround: forward the important bits to your email.
- **Briefing times off by an hour.** Settings > General > Bot time zone. Fix it, then *"read my routines back."*
- **Briefing is a novel.** *"Keep briefings under 8 lines."* It listens. Mostly.
- **Running out of time.** Connect the top three tools, set up the routines, stop. *"resume"* tomorrow; your bot offers to finish Day 3 first.
