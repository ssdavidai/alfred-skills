# Day 2: Map your matters

A butler who is told "help with everything" ends up polishing the wrong silver. Today we work out what your life is actually made of, so your bot knows what to carry, and whether any of it deserves a footman of its own.

**Time:** 15 to 20 minutes of interview with your bot, then 10 to 15 of setup. All in your bot's chat.

## Why it matters

Your company, your clients, the household and the kids' school year all compete for the same brain at 22:00. Split into matters, each one gets its own attention, its own sheet and its own place in the briefing, and nothing from the school app ends up in a client thread. Most dads find the ball they keep dropping within the first five questions.

## What's a matter?

A **matter** is one area of your life or work that keeps generating obligations: a client, a business, a side project, the household, the kids' school year. A task has a deadline. A matter has an arc. Most people land on three to six.

Your butler calls them **life areas**. Same thing; he's just more formal. Each matter becomes its own ledger on Day 5, and your morning briefing reads from all of them. So today's list matters. Pun regretted, not removed.

## Today

1. **Say yes to your bot's morning message** (or *"I'm doing the 7-day challenge, day 2"*). It opens with the interview.
2. **Answer its questions.** About ten, one at a time. Short answers are fine; it will push back if you're vague. Humans say "work stuff" the way cats say "meow": technically communication.
3. **Check the matters it proposes.** Rename, merge or cut anything that's off. Pick two for the first wave. You have the final say. Annoying, but those are the rules.
4. **Nothing to copy.** Your bot saves the summary itself as `matters.md`.
5. **Let your bot read the matters back.** It saves them as your life areas and sets up a small folder per matter. No sheets yet; that's Day 5.
6. **Read the teammate bot brief, if it proposes one.** It flags at most one or two matters that are busy enough (their own inbox or Slack, weekly calls, lots of open items). Your bot writes the brief: scope, sources, routines, and what it may send on its own. That last line should say **nothing**. If it's right, say **"approve this brief"**. One bot today, maximum. The others can wait until Day 7.

Your bot won't ask for health details, passwords or money figures. Call your clients "Client A" if you prefer. It doesn't need their names to know they email you at 23:00.

## Done when

Your bot has sent its "Day 2 done" block: three to six matters saved as life areas, folders created, promise log tagged, and teammate bots either created from a brief you approved, proposed for later, or consciously skipped.

## Snags

- **"I only have two matters."** Your bot will find a third. It's usually admin, and it's usually the one dropping balls.
- **The brief says the bot may send messages.** Say *"change this so it only drafts and never sends on its own."* Then approve.
- **It wants to create three bots.** *"One today. List the rest as proposed."* You can always add more. Removing is the awkward bit.
- **"Do I even need a teammate bot?"** No. Your main butler carries every matter perfectly well. A teammate bot is for the one that has its own inbox and its own weekly rhythm.
- **It tries to connect the matter's Slack or inbox.** *"That's Day 3."* It knows. It's keen.
