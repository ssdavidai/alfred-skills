# Day 1: Set up your butler

You don't need a smarter chatbot. You need someone who remembers, works while you sleep, and asks before touching anything sharp. Today you hire him, and agree the house rules before he touches a single drawer.

**Time:** about 30 minutes, most of it your bot asking you questions.

## Why it matters

A butler who doesn't know you gives advice for a generic person with no children and infinite evenings. Today he learns your name, your time zone, your role and who's at home, so tomorrow's briefing sounds like it was written for your house. And he signs a charter, so you never have to wonder what he'll do while you're at the school gate.

## "Why Grok Bot? I already use Claude / ChatGPT."

Honest answer: it doesn't have to be Grok Bot. Everything in this course (matters, ledgers, the morning briefing) is a set of principles and plain files. This pack is that translation. Claude and ChatGPT can hold it. Grok Bot still can, from the original template.

David picked Grok Bot because the experience fits what he wants from a butler: its own computer and browser, so work keeps going with your laptop shut; connectors to Gmail, Calendar, Sheets, Slack and friends; routines on a schedule or on an event; memory and skills, so you explain things once; desktop and mobile apps talking to the same bot; and draft before send. And there's a ready-made template for this course, so you start with a butler who already knows the job.

If you are in Claude or ChatGPT, you already chose. The steps below are the same job. Where a step needs a computer, a vault, or a routine that only Grok Bot has, your assistant will say so and give you the portable version. It will not pretend.

## Today

1. **Install your butler.** In Claude or ChatGPT, follow the pack's README (the skill zip, or a Project plus the instruction file). On Grok Bot, open the **[Agentic Butler template](https://x.ai/bot/gnwO_M9gMWTJ8xhUzjFi4)** and install **Agentic Butler**. Give it a real name. Not "Assistant". You don't call your plumber "Pipes".
2. **Install the phone app too**, if that product has one, signed in with the same account, notifications on. Approvals and briefings will find you there.
3. **Say to your bot:** *"I'm doing the 7-day challenge."* No file needed; the template already knows every day. Its first question: what time should it message you each morning? [09:00, your time zone]. From tomorrow, that message is your cue.
4. **Answer its questions, one at a time.** First your profile: what to call you, your time zone, your role, your household (roles and first names you're happy to use, kids' ages only if useful), and your preferences (dark or light visuals, language, how short you like replies).
5. **Agree the charter.** It drafts seven rules, starting with *"You draft, I press send."* Edit anything that's off, then say **"approved"**. It reads them back in seven lines.
6. **Let it check what it can reach.** A read-only access list: what's connected, what isn't, what it'll ask for on Day 3. It must not start connecting things today. If it tries, say *"stop, that's Day 3."*
7. **Let tonight's routine run.** At the end of your day it asks one question: *what did you promise today?* Answer it. Quietly, it's the first row of the ledgers you'll build on Day 5.

Need to stop halfway? Say **"pause"**. Later: **"resume"**. Tomorrow morning your bot messages you at the time you chose.

## Done when

Your bot has sent its "Day 1 done" block and the morning kickoff routine is set: name, time zone, profile saved, charter approved and read back, access list written, promise routine tested.

## Proof to post

The seven charter lines your bot read back, pasted as text, plus a screenshot of the "Day 1 done" block. Blur your time zone and anything about your household if you'd rather.

## Snags

- **It asks three questions at once.** Say *"one question at a time, please."* It knows. It got excited.
- **Routine at the wrong hour.** Routines use the time zone in Settings > General > Bot. Fix it there, then *"read the promise routine back and test it."*
- **It jumps ahead to connecting email.** *"Stop, that's Day 3."* Nothing is lost.
- **Unsure how much family to share.** Roles are enough: "partner", "two kids, primary school". You can add more later. Or never. Both are fine.
- **The access list is nearly empty.** Expected. That's Day 3's job.
