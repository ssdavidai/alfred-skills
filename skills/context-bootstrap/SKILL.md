# Context Bootstrap

Optional Day 6 habit. The owner may be ready to work while remembering almost nothing about the project. They will recognise the client or file name without remembering what it means or what they did last time. Assume the working context is gone at the start of each substantial block, unless they say "just the changes".

You remembering the project doesn't mean they do. Reuse what you know. Rebuild the explanation.

## Which block?

- Their explicit choice wins. Otherwise use today's Butler calendar hold and this morning's briefing.
- A calendar entry doesn't prove the previous block happened or finished.
- If two very different blocks are plausible, ask one short question. Don't make them reconstruct history for you.

## Gather privately

Only what's needed to establish:

- **Purpose:** who it's for and what problem it solves.
- **The central thing:** the document, design, system or conversation, and enough of its actual contents to explain it. Open it. A ledger row saying "finish the proposal" can't explain the proposal.
- **What exists:** delivered and verified, versus drafted, planned or untested.
- **The last change:** the last meeting, message or work session, and why it makes this the next task.
- **What's next:** the next real obligation, its date, anyone they're waiting on, and the exact place to resume.

Sources, in this order: the Butler hold's description, the matter's Work rows (by ID), the matter's sources in `sources.md` (the original email, transcript or thread, not just a summary), and the files themselves. Keep observed, inferred and unknown apart. If a source is missing, say which fact is uncertain. Never fill a gap with a plausible story.

## Deliver

Calm, connected prose, built from familiar to specific. Cover, without announcing six headings:

1. **What we're doing and why**, in plain words.
2. **How it works:** the central thing, its parts, and one concrete example walked through.
3. **What you've already done**, and what that makes possible.
4. **Where you left off and what changed.**
5. **What this block is for:** the specific change, who receives it, and the next commitment it serves (with the Work ID).
6. **Where to start:** one action at an exact entry point. A file and section, a screen, a draft paragraph, a decision with its evidence. "Open the project and get going" doesn't count.

Introduce every name and acronym before relying on it ("the product lead, who signs off designs"). No backlog dumps, no tool logs, no motivational speech, no quiz. Length: whatever it takes to restore the model, then stop. Usually 150 to 400 words.

If the remaining work doesn't fit the block, say so and suggest a realistic adjustment. Don't quietly shrink a promise to talking points.

## During and after

- When they pause or switch, keep the actual stopping point, what changed, and the exact next action in this conversation. Offer a two-line restart note only if useful.
- A topic switch is not completion. A draft is not sent.
- This skill doesn't authorise sending, calendar changes, ledger updates or doing the work itself. The nightly ledger run will pick up what actually happened.
