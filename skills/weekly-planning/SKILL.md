# Weekly planning: repeatable progress with slack

Make the important commitments doable while protecting family time, movement and recovery. Leave capacity unused, and make the next work block easy to start. The owner's current instructions override these defaults.

## Working sequence
1. **Carry forward decisions.** Keep a short working record of the owner's latest constraints, effort estimates, deferrals and what they've authorized.
2. **Set the fixed edges.** Check local time and every relevant calendar before proposing anything. Check important promises against their sources.
3. **Reserve capacity first.** Place appointments, family commitments, travel, lunch, transitions and protected resets before any demanding work. Use last week's structure and what actually happened then.
4. **Allocate outcomes.** Work backward from delivery dates. Estimate remaining effort separately from how much free calendar time there is.
5. **Stress-test and present.** State the outcomes, open dependencies, finish times and any delivery gap. Mark proposed placements clearly as different from confirmed commitments.
6. **Execute only when authorized.** Apply the agreed calendar changes, check that they saved, and report what changed. Don't ask for the same permission twice.

## Capacity defaults (the owner can change these)
- Start serious work mid-morning. Keep about an hour for lunch and a reset, moving it around appointments if needed.
- Late afternoon is the usual latest finish, not a target. Don't assume evenings or weekends are available for catch-up.
- At most two substantial demanding blocks a day, often fewer. Two is a ceiling, not a quota.
- Allow 10–20 minutes to get into context and 10–15 minutes to capture and close, inside or next to each block.
- Keep resets separate from work. Email, prep and checking on agents are work.
- Protect family, childcare, medical and travel commitments. Never assume someone else covers childcare.
- Physical chores and travel use up capacity too. Don't treat them as free time or as automatic recovery.

## Readiness without diagnosis
Use what the owner says now plus recent evidence. Being productive doesn't prove they're well, and a gap in messages doesn't prove they rested. On a strained week, plan fewer contexts, longer transitions and earlier finishes.

## When the day changes
Refresh only the evidence that's affected. Keep the essentials and move or drop the rest. Say plainly what no longer fits.

## Rules
Planning gives permission to read and recommend, nothing more. Calendar writes, messages and automations need explicit approval.
